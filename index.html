Most of the ocean has never been seen. We go and look.

Satellites see the surface. Ship sonar sees the floor. Between them is 1.3 billion cubic kilometres of water that is measured in slices and guessed in between. We fill the gaps with instruments that stay down for months and vehicles that go where the pressure forbids people.

Every survey is published raw within ninety days: pressure, temperature, salinity, oxygen, acoustic backscatter and video, registered to a shared depth grid so that one institute's twilight-zone record lines up with another's.

The descent through five light zones

0 m2001 0004 0006 00011 000

  1. 0 – 200 m

    Sunlight

    Where photosynthesis is possible. Almost all of the ocean's fishery and nearly all of what people picture when they picture the sea.

  2. 200 – 1 000 m

    Twilight

    Light fades to a blue memory. The largest animal migration on Earth happens here every night, as billions of tonnes of life rise to feed and sink at dawn.

  3. 1 000 – 4 000 m

    Midnight

    No sunlight at all. Temperatures near 4 °C, pressure past 100 atmospheres. Our moorings record the slow snow of organic matter that feeds everything below.

  4. 4 000 – 6 000 m

    Abyss

    The abyssal plain covers more than half the planet's surface. Flat, cold, and so sparsely sampled that a single lander deployment can describe a region the size of a country.

  5. 6 000 – 11 000 m

    Hadal

    The trenches. Pressure above 1 100 atmospheres crushes most electronics; ours are oil-filled and pressure-balanced. Our deepest recovery came back from 10 902 m with four days of video.
